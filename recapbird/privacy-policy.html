Privacy Policy — RecapBird

App: “RecapBird” on Google Play (recapbird.ai.note.taker.voice.recorder.summary) · Developer: KLapp

Effective date: October 14, 2026

This policy explains what data the RecapBird Android app collects, uses and shares, why, how long it is kept, and what you can do about it. It describes the current version of the app, which shows a built-in sample note and the app's screens. “We” means KLapp, the developer of RecapBird.

1. Who we are

RecapBird is published on Google Play by the developer KLapp, which decides how the data described here is used (the “controller”). Privacy contact and support: applarik+recapbird@gmail.com

You never create an account with us or sign in. The app does not ask for your name, e-mail address or phone number.

2. What stays on your phone

  • The sample note is built into the app. When you copy or share its text, Android's clipboard or share sheet handles it and we receive nothing.
  • The app's own records, kept only on your phone: whether you have finished the first launch, which version of the app's introduction you see (a random letter, A to D), and a record of your tap on “Get started” (section 5): which version of the welcome text you saw, the version of these documents, the four Google consent settings described in section 5 (all allowed), the time and the app version. This version of the app sends none of these records to us or to anyone else.
  • Permissions. Through Google's software (section 3) the app uses the internet and the advertising ID. It has no access to your microphone, your device location (GPS), your contacts, or your photos and files; Google Analytics estimates your approximate location from your IP address instead (section 3).
  • Android's backup and phone-to-phone transfer leave out everything the app keeps, so uninstalling the app removes it from the phone for good. Data already sent to Google is kept as section 7 describes.

3. Services in the app

The app contains Google's Firebase software. When the app runs, that software sends the data listed under the first three services below to Google, which processes it for us; the app itself sends nothing else to us or to anyone else. The other parties below receive data in connection with the app. While running Crashlytics and Cloud Messaging, Google also collects technical data such as IP addresses and the app's identifiers (“Firebase Service Data”) and uses it under its own Privacy Policy to run, secure and improve its services (https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

Google Analytics for Firebase

  • What it receives, and why: an app-instance ID; the Android advertising ID; how the app was found on Google Play (the Play Store install referrer, for example the campaign of an ad or a link); your approximate location, derived from your IP address (Google states that Google Analytics does not log or store IP addresses); phone model, Android version, language and app version; and the events Google Analytics records on its own, such as the first open, sessions, engagement time, screen views, app updates and removals, and crashes. The app logs no events of its own. To understand how many people install and use the app, and to measure our ads (section 4).
  • Role and terms: Google Analytics Terms of Service (https://marketingplatform.google.com/about/analytics/terms/us/); Google processes the data for us. Under the data-sharing options turned on in section 4, Google may also use it for modeling and business insights, for technical support and for recommendations to us.

Firebase Crashlytics

  • What it receives, and why: when the app crashes or has a serious error, the stack trace, the state of the app, phone model and Android version, a random Crashlytics installation ID, the Firebase installation ID and the Analytics events just before the crash. In addition, each time you open the app, even without a crash, Firebase's sessions software, which Crashlytics uses, sends app and phone details (such as the app's package name, Android version, phone maker and model, and network connection type) and when the app came to the foreground. To find and fix crashes and measure how often they happen.
  • Role and terms: Google acts for us (https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

Firebase installations and Cloud Messaging

  • What it receives, and why: a Firebase installation ID, a random ID of this installation of the app (it contains no name and no hardware ID of your phone, but it singles out this installation), which Crashlytics also uses; and a notification token with the app version, which Google's messaging software in the app creates on its own. This version of the app sends you no notifications, does not ask for permission to show them and does not use the token.
  • Role and terms: Google acts for us.

Google BigQuery

  • What it receives, and why: a daily copy of the Google Analytics events described above, with the details Google Analytics stores for each event (including the app-instance ID, the advertising ID, approximate location, phone and app details, and how the app was found), in our own Google Cloud project in the United States, so that we keep the raw events of the app's first users for analysis.
  • Role and terms: Google acts for us; each day's copy is deleted after 60 days (section 7).

Gmail, support tools and Telegram

  • What they receive, and why: e-mails you send us, which arrive in our Gmail mailbox and are copied to computers our team runs; Telegram receives only the sender address, subject, date and mailbox folder of each e-mail, to notify our team (section 8).
  • Role and terms: Gmail is Google's e-mail service, under Google's terms and Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy); the AI assistant we use for support works under its provider's terms; Telegram is a messaging platform under the Telegram Privacy Policy (https://telegram.org/privacy).

Authorities

  • What the law requires, only where required.

The app shows no ads and contains no ad network software; it uses Google Analytics to measure our ads (section 4). We do not sell your data for money. Using Google Analytics to measure and personalize our ads may count as “selling” or “sharing” under some US state laws (section 14).

4. Analytics and advertising features

Our Google Analytics property has ads personalization turned on. Of Google Analytics’ data-sharing options, “Modeling contributions & business insights”, “Technical support” and “Recommendations for your business” are turned on. Our Google Analytics property is not linked to a Google Ads account. Google Analytics records the Android advertising ID together with the app-instance ID. How Google uses data from apps that use its services: https://business.safety.google/privacy/.

Google Analytics, Crashlytics and the other Firebase software in section 3 start working when the app first opens, before you reach the “Get started” button.

5. What you accept at “Get started”

The first screen of the app says: “By starting you accept our Terms of Use and Privacy Policy, including ads measurement and personalization (Google, Apphud).” Tapping Get started accepts these terms and this policy, including the use of data from the app for measuring and personalizing our ads described in section 4. Apphud, a subscription service, is named in that line; the current version of the app contains no Apphud software and sends Apphud no data.

From the first launch, Google Analytics treats four consent settings as allowed: storing data for analytics, storing data for advertising, using data for advertising, and ad personalization. The app records your tap on your phone (section 2). This version of the app has no setting to change these choices inside the app; section 6 explains what you can do instead.

6. Your choices

  • Advertising ID: in Android, open Settings → Privacy → Ads. “Delete advertising ID” deletes it and no new one is assigned; “Reset advertising ID” replaces it with a new one and does not stop its use. On some older versions, open Settings → Privacy → Advanced → Ads, then “Opt out of Ads Personalization”. Google's software in the app follows these settings. Google states that resetting the advertising ID also resets Google Analytics' app-instance ID. Neither step deletes data already collected; it is kept as section 7 describes.
  • Google's ad settings: https://adssettings.google.com.
  • Uninstalling RecapBird stops all collection by the app at once.
  • Objections and other requests: write to us (sections 9 and 14).

7. How long data is kept

  • On your phone: until you uninstall the app or clear its storage in Android's settings.
  • Google Analytics: we set Google Analytics to keep user-level and event-level data for 14 months; Google then deletes it automatically in a monthly run, and for user-level data it counts the 14 months from your last use of the app. This setting does not limit Google's aggregated reports.
  • BigQuery copy: each day's copy of Analytics events is deleted automatically after 60 days; Google keeps deleted copies in its recovery storage for up to 14 more days.
  • Crashlytics: keeps crash details and the related IDs for 90 days, then starts removing them from its live and backup systems. Google publishes no separate period for the sessions data that Crashlytics uses for its crash statistics (section 3).
  • Firebase installation ID: kept by Firebase until a request to delete it reaches Firebase, then removed from its live and backup systems within 180 days; this version of the app sends no such request. Notification token: Firebase treats it as expired and deletes it once the app has not connected for 270 days.
  • Support e-mails: kept, with their copies on our computers and the Telegram notifications about them, until you ask us to delete them.
  • Data Google holds as a controller in its own right follows Google's Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy).

8. Support e-mail

  • We receive your e-mail address, your name if your e-mail shows it, your message, attachments and anything you include.
  • Handling. Support e-mails arrive in our Gmail mailbox and are copied to computers our team runs; our team reads them, sometimes with the help of an AI assistant. When an e-mail arrives, its sender address, subject, date and mailbox folder are sent to our team as a Telegram notification; the message text and attachments are not. A person answers every e-mail by hand; no reply is sent automatically.
  • Use. Only to answer you and handle your request. We never add your address to a mailing list or use it for advertising.

9. Deleting your data

  • On your phone. Uninstall RecapBird, or open Android Settings → Apps → RecapBird → Storage & cache and tap Clear storage (the names vary by phone). Either way, everything the app keeps on the phone is deleted, including the IDs of Google's services stored there; the app has no account to delete.
  • Data held by Google for us. This version of the app has no in-app data deletion. Analytics and crash data are deleted automatically after the periods in section 7; the Firebase installation ID is not (section 7). You can write to us at applarik+recapbird@gmail.com to ask for deletion; you do not need the app to do so. A person reads every e-mail and answers by hand. The app has no account and shows you no ID, so an e-mail by itself does not tell us which analytics or crash records are yours, and we do not delete records on the strength of an e-mail alone. If you also send details that let us identify your records, we delete them; if we cannot identify your data, we tell you so.
  • Support e-mails. We delete your e-mails to us, their copies and the Telegram notifications about them when you ask from the address that sent them.
  • Our deletion page sums this up: https://klapp-app.github.io/recapbird/delete-data.html.

10. Why we use data, and legal bases (EEA and UK)

  • Storing or reading information on your phone for advertising; ad personalization; measuring our ads and building audiences with Google (section 4). Legal basis: your consent, given by tapping “Get started” (section 5).
  • Analytics, crash reports, the Firebase sessions data, the BigQuery copy, and the installation ID and notification token that Google's software creates (section 3). Legal basis: our legitimate interests in keeping the app working and improving it, in knowing how it is used, and in using Google's Firebase software, which creates these IDs on its own; you can object by writing to us, and uninstalling the app stops all collection.
  • Keeping the record of your tap on “Get started” on your phone. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in keeping a record of what you accepted and when; this version keeps it only on your phone.
  • Answering e-mails and requests; lawful requests. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in answering you, and our legal obligations.

You are not required by law or by contract to give us personal data. Google's software in the app sends the data in section 3 whenever the app runs; if you do not want that, do not use the app, or uninstall it.

11. International transfers

Google (Analytics, Firebase, BigQuery, Gmail) processes data in the United States and other countries; our BigQuery copy is stored in the United States. Google relies on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and its UK and Swiss extensions (https://www.dataprivacyframework.gov/list). Telegram and the AI assistant we use for support receive support e-mails or their details (sections 3 and 8) and may process them in countries outside the EEA and the UK for which no adequacy decision exists; we pass them only what our team needs to notice and answer your e-mail. You can ask us for information about these transfers at applarik+recapbird@gmail.com.

12. Security

Google encrypts the data its Analytics and Firebase software sends from the app in transit (HTTPS/TLS). Access to our Analytics, Firebase and Google Cloud data is limited to our team and to Google, which processes it for us. Support e-mails are handled only by our team and the services named in section 3. Android's backup and phone-to-phone transfer leave out the app's data (section 2). No method is perfectly secure.

13. Children

RecapBird is meant for adults aged 18 and over and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 18. If you believe a child has used the app or sent us personal data, write to us and we will delete what we can find.

14. Your rights

EEA and UK: you can ask to access, correct, delete, restrict, object to or port your personal data, withdraw consent at any time, and complain to your data protection authority. Withdrawing consent does not affect the lawfulness of processing based on your consent before you withdrew it. To withdraw consent, write to us: this version of the app has no setting for it, and uninstalling the app stops all further collection. You can object at any time to our use of your data for direct marketing, including related profiling, by writing to us. United States: depending on your state, you may have the right to know, access, correct and delete your personal information, and to opt out of its “sale” or “sharing” for targeted advertising, without being treated differently. To opt out, or to withdraw consent, write to us; uninstalling the app stops all further collection. Android's advertising ID controls (section 6) are additional and do not by themselves exercise these rights. Because the app has no account, we may be unable to link a request to the data collected from your phone; we then tell you so. To use your rights or appeal our decision on a request, write to applarik+recapbird@gmail.com; where the law allows, an authorized agent may ask for you. We reply within one month.
